Interview prep · Part 3

Behavioral: STAR

Tell a clear story about what you did.

Behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time when...") test how you work with people and handle trouble. STAR keeps your answer short and clear.

  • S: Situation. One or two sentences of context.
  • T: Task. What you were responsible for.
  • A: Action. What you did, step by step. This is most of the answer.
  • R: Result. What happened, with a number if you have one, and what you learned.

Template

Situation: At [place/project], [one-line context].
Task:      I was responsible for [goal], and the challenge was [obstacle].
Action:    First I [step 1]. Then I [step 2]. When [problem] came up,
           I [step 3].
Result:    As a result, [outcome, with a number if possible].
           What I learned: [takeaway].

Build a story bank

Don't memorize answers. Prepare five or six real stories and map each to several questions:

  • A project you're proud of
  • A mistake or failure, and what changed after
  • A disagreement with a teammate
  • Leading or organizing people
  • Learning something hard, fast
  • Working under a tight deadline

Tips

  • Say "I," not "we." They're hiring you.
  • Aim for about two minutes.
  • Be honest about failures. Spend most of the time on what you did next.
  • Practice out loud, ideally with a friend.

A worked skeleton

Question: Tell me about a time you led a team.

S: Our club had members but no shared project.
T: I volunteered to organize a team to build one.
A: I split the idea into small tasks, assigned them by interest,
   set a weekly check-in, and reviewed every change.
R: We shipped [what], and [how many] people contributed.
   I learned that clear small tasks make it easy to join in.

Swap in your own details. A real, specific story beats a polished made-up one.
