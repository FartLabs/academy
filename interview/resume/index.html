Interview prep · Part 1

Your resume

One page that makes someone want to talk to you.

A resume has one job: get you the conversation. Keep it to one page, as a PDF, with no photo and no fancy layout. A recruiter spends seconds on it, so make those seconds count.

Order

  1. Header: name, email, GitHub, and a link to your site or best project.
  2. Experience: internships, jobs, and club leadership count. Newest first.
  3. Projects: your strongest evidence if you have little experience. Link a live demo.
  4. Education: school, degree, and graduation date.
  5. Skills: languages and tools you could actually discuss.

Write bullets with a formula

Say what you accomplished, how you measured it, and what you did to get there.

Accomplished [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z].

Compare these two. The second tells the reader what changed.

Weak:   Worked on the club website.
Better: Rebuilt the club website in plain HTML and CSS, cutting
        load time from 4s to under 1s for 200 members.

Those numbers are examples. Use only your real results. If you can't measure it, describe the outcome in plain words.

Checklist

  • Starts each bullet with a strong verb (built, led, shipped, cut).
  • Every project has a link that works.
  • You can talk for two minutes about anything on the page.
  • A friend read it and found no typos.
  • Tailored a little for each role: reorder bullets so the most relevant come first.
